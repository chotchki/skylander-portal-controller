## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Eruptor fights the original's first fight on the Mac, every combat rule coming from the original's scripts on the user's disc, checked against the oracle in the layers decision 15 lists.
- The experience and gold a kid earns land on that kid's profile the moment they're collected and show on the TV and the phone; the phone edits and resets them.
- A working VM core with its member count measured, so AJ starts from running script data, not from a plan.
- Every native stand-in AH leaves is a requirement in the stand-in table with the rung that deletes it.

**Non-Goals:**
- The rest of level 213's scripts: the pit's other four encounter sets, Eon's lines, the tutorial prompts, the intro cinematic, the camera pans (AJ).
- Upgrades, the upgrade screen, Magma Ball, Wow Pow, hats and heroics (AK; ah-progression has the upgrade costs, 500-5,000).
- Titan feats: the boulders, the emeralds under them, any wall only a Giant breaks (a backlog line).
- Particles and combat sound (no particle decoder, no FSB5 decode in guacamole; backlog lines). Effects with no model on disc get a plain stand-in shape.
- The original's HUD, menus, story saves, accolades and achievements (AM). Damage numbers unless AH.7-AH.9's shots show the original draws them by default.
- Co-op (AL) and PvP (AI): one hero, as AG left it.
- Windows; the couch (the TV pass).

## Decisions

**3. The script runtime: a pure crate, written from a spec in our words.**
New crate `crates/guac-script` (no Bevy, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`), the op layouts in `guac-formats` (aj-script section 6.1: the 44 op classes on disc and the metaobject-index resolution, each a property test and a fuzz target). Its semantics are written down first, in our words, in the crate's docs and tests, from aj-vm and ah-progression, the decompile only explaining what they show: a program counter over each placement's op array stepped once a logic tick with no yields (relative forward skips; 21,774 else-if skips cross the end of their block on disc, so a PC, not a tree walk); a block stack; behaviours as named states; macros as static frames with a busy guard; structures, sets and arithmetic; comparison codes `0 <=, 1 ==, 2 >=, 3 <, 4 >, 5 !=` (not the Wii doc's table; negation pairs 0↔4, 1↔5, 2↔3; ah-progression's reading, which AH.9's level-up at exactly 1,000 confirms or breaks) with a 1e-4 tolerance on floats; casts truncating; `random(N)` per decision 9; distances in feet (the distance member returns metres × 3.2808, so a script's 7 is 2.13 m). Member cells are typed from the static registry (AC.4: each member's measurement type, units and min/max), converting and clamping as registered. Two modes: strict (an unmodelled member stops the placement's script and names the member, the test mode) and value-cell (it reads its registered default and keeps what's written, the play mode for cosmetic members only, each listed). The VM steps in `FixedUpdate` at AG's 1/60 s game step; its tick order against the hero's controller and the camera, and the scheduler's groups, come from oracle traces of each actor's behaviour (AH.8), not the reading. Fixes ship as our code in the VM or a member, never as edits to the data (Method, step 4).

Rejected: a hand-written rule engine shaped like the macros (rewriting the data); transliterating the EBOOT's interpreter (the Method); waiting for AJ (decision 2).

**4. Members and world operations: reached, written, counted.**
- Movement: the per-frame velocity model is shared by every actor; with no stick an actor goes the AI way, through its destination and waypoints (M, ag-motion). So a Chompy is AG.16's controller fed a destination instead of a stick, its speed, acceleration, deceleration and turn rate from its scripts (20 ft/s, 160, 80, 720°/s, M); `guac-motion` grows the destination input with its own unit tests, the hero's path unchanged.
- Sequences by palette slot with time and percent remaining, on AE's evaluator and AG.14's crossfade; a script's "fire when 84 % of the clip is left at 150 %" reads the clip's own length from the extract (decision 5).
- Spawning clones a template (`OpSpawn`; AG.7 counted 80 clones at AF's fresh state), plus remove, displace, turn, move and teleport.
- Contacts, overlaps, rays and field-of-view checks go through AG.4's seam; a lobbed projectile is a kinematic actor its script moves (lob speed, pitch, gravity), its hits a cast.
- The player loop (decision 2's first row): a native rule that, every 0.25 s per player, calls the original's pickup macros, and on defeat its death-sequence macro, so the macros run as shipped and only the scheduling is ours.
- AG's stand-ins stay: the camera (viewport members are value cells, so the Norticus pan does nothing), the start's rule for every placement off the whitelist, the entry flow.
Each member lands with a unit test from its registered type, units and range; every box that adds members records the count.

**5. Script data, palettes and models in the extract, by parts.**
- Scripts: the decompressed IGZ entries that hold the whitelist's scripts (level 213's `level.bld`, `global.bld`'s, `init_setup.bld`'s and each fighting character's), copied raw into the extract as a part of their own (AG.15's per-part rule), parsed by `guac-formats` at level load behind AG.19's background load. A script refers to placements, structures and sets in the same file, so a subset isn't self-contained; whole entries it is. Rejected: (b) reading the dump at level load (LZMA and the IGA reader on the load path, and a second runtime source beside the extract); (c) an IR for 44 op classes and their shared-object graph (a second schema kept in step with the reader for one consumer). This departs from the design doc's "tfbScript graphs → IR", and AH.21 rewrites that line with the reason; it's revisited if load time or the cache's size argues.
- Palettes: every animation palette slot of each fighting character and each creature a whitelisted placement names (AG.14 took 0-3, 57 and 61; Eruptor's throw is 20 and 21 and his eruption 32, the Chompy's 6, 11 and 20-25), and the models of what the whitelist spawns where the disc has them (the lava ball, orbs, coins, debris).
- New records in new files, so `IR_VERSION` stays 3 and `LAYOUT_VERSION` 4 and an extract resumes for the new parts; if a record AH touches changes shape, IR v4 re-extracts once; AH.12 records which. The stage's time and size get measured (the first run's delay ahead of intros, AG decision 8's accounting).

**6. The figure store: one row per profile per figure, written the moment it changes, one writer.**
- `figure_instances` in guacamole's own set: `profile_id` (→ `profiles`, on delete cascade), `figure_id` as `figure_usage` keys it, `xp` 0-96,500 as the total (never the original's 33,000/63,500 split), `gold` 0-65,000, `playtime_secs`, `created_at`, `updated_at`; key (profile, figure). It holds what AH needs of the list a fresh instance must eventually hold (ah-progression section 1: upgrades, hat, heroics and accolades come with the rungs that use them). The store's queries live on guacamole's arm only, so the product's frozen set never sees the table and the both-sets test still passes.
- Fresh at a profile's first placement, beside `record_figure_usage`; the native `Placement` carries the instance's progress to the TV. A placement with no owner makes no row: its progress lives for the session in the TV alone.
- Written through: every change (an orb, a coin, a level-up, a second of playtime batched per second) is one row update. The server's store is the only writer: the TV sends each change over a bounded channel to the server thread (the portal channel's reverse), the store writes and broadcasts `FigureUpdated`, and the phone re-fetches its own profile's stats on it, so the event needs no new field.
- Rejected: the original's save points (a tag save at level-up, each 100 gold and wherever AH.9's write stamps show): a crash between them loses progress. Writing through is a departure only a crash could see, covered by the design doc's "figure progression lives in Guacamole's store"; AH.9 still records the original's timing.

**7. The figure in the scripts: a buffer in the disc's own layout, never a file.**
The scripts read and write a figure's data through the save-param table `init_setup.bld` builds (24 rows of word, bit, width and limit, M) and bit-field macros over the tag object's engine members. So each instance gets an in-memory buffer in the layout that table describes, built from the row at placement (experience split into the table's two areas, gold, playtime, the rest a fresh figure's zeros) with the tag members answering over it; after each logic tick the TV reads experience (both areas summed, as the game's own total macro does), gold and playtime back and sends any change (decision 6). The scripts' save macros run untouched and the store sees every change at once whatever their save points. No file, no encryption, no checksum and no `sky-parser`: the layout is the disc's table, read statically (AH.2), and nothing in the buffer reaches a `.sky` or RPCS3, so the full reset stands. Rejected: native members at the portal-param level replacing the save macros (rewriting script logic).

**8. The phone's editor on the rows.**
guacamole's arms of `/api/profiles/:profile_id/figures/:figure_id/{stats,edit,reset}` and `/api/portal/slot/:n/reset`, keyed on the `FigureFiles` hook's absence as AA's Native table does:
- Stats from the row, level by the experience table out of the extract's progression part (AH.2); a figure the profile never placed answers 404, the phone's existing never-placed state; before Giants' extract holds the table, 409 as today.
- Edit sets experience to the level's threshold and the gold, refused (409) while the viewer's own placement holds the figure (AA.18's gate, unchanged), and refused (403) when the path's profile isn't the requesting session's unlocked profile: the product's routes take the path's profile at face value behind the HMAC, which PINs as anti-sibling don't cover. A never-placed figure's first edit makes its row.
- Reset deletes the row; the slot reset deletes it and re-places the slot's figure fresh, so a hero in the level carries on at level 1.
- Each broadcasts `FigureUpdated`. Under guacamole (`/api/status`'s `app`) the phone caps every Giants-playable figure at 15, the game's own cap through the spill area (ah-progression); the product keeps its 10. Playtime isn't editable.

**9. Randomness: the original's generator, written down here.**
One MT19937 with the 1998 `sgenrand` seeding (two `69069x + 1` steps a word, AF.8) per session, seeded from the clock, every script roll drawn from it; tests seed it, and `guac_oracle::rng` is the reference. `random(N)`: a whole number uniform on [0, N] with both ends, a fraction on [0, N) (M, aj-vm). The sequence itself can't be compared (the oracle's particles draw on host time), so the ranges and their inclusiveness are what's specified (script-runtime: Random rolls); the same generator lets a seeded headless run line up draw for draw with an oracle replay reseeded through `rng seed`, inside the few frames before the first host-timed draw. guacamole draws nothing for effects, so its draw counts differ by design. The design doc gave this to AJ "when its VM first draws"; AH's VM is first.

**10. Difficulty and the console save's values: the original's defaults, as parameters.**
The original keeps the difficulty (default medium), the portal-master experience multiplier (1.0 with no collection accolades) and the Upgrade-O-Tron's bonus in the console save, which guacamole doesn't have until AM. AH plays medium (the new-save default; AF.8's anchor reads the medium numbers live, `Enemy_Chompy_DirectDMG` 11 and `PandoranGrunt_DMG` 36, where the June state read easy, 4 and 14), the multiplier at 1.0 and no Upgrade-O-Tron, each a parameter tests set to the anchor's values (AH.4 reads them), so a comparison matches whatever an anchor holds. Open question 4.

**11. Breakables and coins.**
About a dozen breakables touch the clearing's reach, plus the ledge route's three, all on the whitelist. The level passes 0 for every destructible drop rate and the loot macro reads 0 as "use the default", 0.6 common, 1 uncommon, 1 rare (M), so breaking them probably drops common coins worth 1, 1 or 2, picked by a random float against equal weights (H until AH.7 breaks one). Gold in reach besides: three spinning coins on the walkway's 9 m drop and three on the fall line, 1 each. Out of reach: Copper Coins and an Emerald 10 m above anything reachable, the two Emeralds under Titan boulders (Giants only) and `Chest(1)` 13.7 m off with no solids at all. What stays solid after a break, and whether any breakable needs a Giant, is AH.7's. Titan-only things stay put for everyone until a Titan feats change (a backlog line AH.21 adds).

**12. Defeat and the HUD.**
- Defeat follows the original: the death sequence (enemies within 30 ft knocked back), the figure out for the level with nothing lost, "Need Remove" if put back, and another character taking over (AG's One hero at a time). Four Chompies at medium can take Eruptor's 290 health in about 15 s of standing still (A: one 11-14 bite per Chompy per 2.7 s or more), so a kid will see it, and with the phone offering every character there's always another to place. When none waits, a one-button screen in AP's theme and router offers Restart chapter, which restarts the level with every figure's progress kept: the original's own defeat screen is AM's menus.
- The HUD is a thin stand-in drawn by the TV in AP's theme (`TvTheme`, `Pen`, bevy_ui): health against full, level, experience toward the next and gold, plus the low-health warning under 20 %. The original's HUD is interface scripts over sprite members, the biggest member namespace (18 % of the disc's references), so drawing it is AM-sized and the stand-in goes then. Rejected: the phone alone (a kid can't see his health mid-fight); a respawn at the last safe point (a departure).

**13. Who fights.**

Open question 3.

**14. The oracle side: readers, O6, a route driver and two anchors.**
- Readers (AH.4), AG.7's pattern: a placement's script variables by name (health, the alerted flag, the current behaviour) off its script instance; the loaded figure's experience and gold as the game holds them (the tag data through AH.2's rows); the level's live enemy stats (ah-combat's live-values reader); the difficulty; the portal-master multiplier; the element-area flag at the hero. Giants' watch list grows by them.
- O6 (AH.5): projectiles, orbs, coins and debris appear after a recording starts, at addresses O5's fixed ranges can't name. A game-agnostic verb follows a pointer chain the harness names (a base, offsets, a list's count and items) at O5's sample point each flip and copies named ranges from each item into O5's ring; Giants' chain is AF.9's actor list and each corpus's transform, velocity, placement and behaviour cell. Rejected: discovering them at a pause after they spawn (the stretch after it moves, AF.11), kept as the fallback for the first measurements if O6 lags.
- The anchors (AH.6): a closed-loop route driver in the harness (pause every few flips, read the hero and camera, steer and press through O4 toward the next waypoint, settle still) then `SAVESTATE`.
