## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Eruptor fights the original's first fight on the Mac, every combat rule coming from the original's scripts on the user's disc, checked against the oracle in the layers decision 18 lists.
- The experience and gold a kid earns land on that kid's profile the moment they're collected, survive guacamole closing straight after, and show on the TV and the phone; the phone edits and resets them.
- A working VM core with its member count measured against a ceiling recounted over every root it runs, so AJ starts from running script data, not from a plan.
- Every native stand-in AH leaves is a requirement in the stand-in table with the rung that deletes it.

**Non-Goals:**
- The rest of level 213's scripts: the pit's other four encounter sets, Eon's lines, the tutorial prompts, the intro cinematic, the camera pans (AJ).
- Upgrades, the upgrade screen, Magma Ball, Wow Pow, hats and heroics (AK; ah-progression has the upgrade costs, 500-5,000).
- Titan feats: the boulders, the emeralds under them, any wall only a Giant breaks (a backlog line).
- Particles and combat sound (no particle decoder, no FSB5 decode in guacamole; backlog lines). Effects with no model on disc get a plain stand-in shape.
- The original's HUD, menus, story saves, accolades and achievements (AM; the HUD a backlog line). Damage numbers unless AH.9-AH.11's shots show the original draws them by default.
- Co-op (AL) and PvP (AI): one hero, as AG left it. The orb range's 100 ft with two figures out is AL's.
- Level and gold on the portal view's cards: the TV's HUD and the phone show them (decision 10).
- Windows; the couch (the TV pass).

## Decisions

**1. The first fight is the original's, in five slices.**
Level 213's encounter sets run Setup_001 (Chompy 1-4 bullying Norticus), Setup_002 (the "Chompie pit", twenty more one a second, then a Grunt), Setup_003 and Setup_004 (more Chompies, two Grunts) and Setup_005 (undead), every Grunt the Simple type (M, ah-combat). Setup_001 is the first a kid meets. Its four stand on the pit floor at y 22.37-22.38 within 5.1 m of each other, 70.5-75.1 m across the ground from the spawn (10.19, 43.38, −93.39) and 21.0 m below it (A); they're invulnerable, state-immune and knockback-resistant (500) until a hero is within 7 vertical feet (2.13 m) of `Enemy_Chompy(1)` (M), so the fight starts when the hero is down in the pit. Around them: `Setup_001_Trigger` (a 30 × 5 × 30 ft box on the upper level), `Activate_Norticus and Chompies`, a camera pan to Norticus (`Eon_4_help_Norticus`), three spinning coins descending into the pit (a fall line, H) and a spike gate opening on Norticus' camera target. The placements name `ChompyUndead.mdl` and the template `Chompy.mdl`; which draws is the anchor shot's to say.

The route from AG's clearing, by AG.7's reach rerun with more variants ([`reach3.txt`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ah-propose/out/reach3.txt), A): every solid standing (AG's walk), the reach stops 54.9 m across and 27.2 m above `Enemy_Chompy(1)`; with every breakable gone, 12.9 m across and 23.8 m above; with the three `Push_Block_Template`s gone too, 9.1 m across and directly over the pit, 1.1 m across the ground from its spike gate and 23.2 m above it. **3. The script runtime: a pure crate, written from a spec in our words.**
New crate `crates/guac-script` (no Bevy, `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]`), the op layouts and a graph writer in `guac-formats` (aj-script section 6.1: the 44 op classes on disc and the metaobject-index resolution, each a property test and a fuzz target). Its semantics are written down first, in our words, in the crate's docs and tests, from aj-vm and ah-progression, the decompile only explaining what they show: a program counter over each placement's op array stepped once a logic tick with no yields (relative forward skips; 21,774 else-if skips cross the end of their block on disc, so a PC, not a tree walk); a block stack; behaviours as named states; macros as static frames with a busy guard; structures, sets and arithmetic in single precision; comparison codes `0 <=, 1 ==, 2 >=, 3 <, 4 >, 5 !=` (not the Wii doc's table; negation pairs 0↔4, 1↔5, 2↔3; ah-progression's reading, which AH.11's level-up at exactly 1,000 confirms or breaks) with a 1e-4 tolerance on floats; casts truncating; `random(N)` per decision 11; distances in feet (the distance member returns metres × 3.2808, so a script's 7 is 2.13 m). Member cells are typed from the static registry (AC.4: each member's measurement type, units and min/max), converting and clamping as registered.
- Because it steps disc data with no yields, a wrong skip or a malformed graph could loop inside one tick: a per-placement, per-tick op budget, set above the largest tick the disc tests measure (aj-vm's tool ran `Init_Setup.ai`'s whole first tick in 57,418 op executions), stops that placement's script and names it. A cargo-fuzz target drives the interpreter itself over graphs from the writer's shapes, not only the readers.
- Two modes. Strict (the test mode): an unmodelled member stops the placement's script and names the member. Play: the cosmetic members listed in each member box are value cells (they read the registered default and keep what's written); any other unmodelled member stops that placement's script with a log line naming it and the placement (never a panic) while the rest of the world runs on.
- A value cell can stall a script that waits for the engine to animate it (`Game - Award Experience`, `Game - Award Money` and `Destructibles - On Hurt` read `colormeasurement.alpha`). So each member box lists every value cell with whether any whitelisted branch reads it, and the headless runner carries a stall detector: a whitelisted placement whose behaviour the oracle's trace shows advancing must advance within that behaviour's longest recorded stay.
- The VM steps in `FixedUpdate` at AG's 1/60 s game step. Its tick order against the hero's controller and the camera comes from what separates the orders in AH.10's traces: a Chompy's facing at frame N against the hero's pose at N and at N−1 while the hero turns, or a bite's contact against this frame's and last frame's hero position. O5 samples once a frame at the pad read, so it can't see inside a frame; if the traces can't separate the orders, the order is labelled a hypothesis from the reading, as AG.16 does for its unobservables. AH.10 separated them: in 234 of 236 steps where a battling Chompy turned to the hero under its 12° a frame while a push carried him (3 m/s or more, the two aims 2° or more apart, within 6 m), its facing landed on where he stood before that frame's movement (to 0.00°) and missed where he stood after it by 1.9-13.8°; and a bite's hit reads at the frame's starting positions (5.54-6.01 ft between middles over 68 bites, against 5.70-7.34 a record later). So the scripts tick before the hero's movement in a frame. Among the placements AH.10's encounter end separates the orders too: Norticus takes his scene the frame the last Chompy goes and `Setup_001_Controller`, which checks the same, goes a frame later, which ticking in the order added misses by a frame both ways and the order they woke in (the controller first, the Chompies, then Norticus) gives exactly (AH.18). So placements tick in the order they woke, one woken after every placement already awake. AH.59 then read the original's scheduler a frame at a time on the oracle: the hero's minion alone in its first group, ticking ahead of every other placement, then the awake placements in the order they woke, the mover update at their end every frame (the music controller's tick resets it), then the actors moving; guacamole's fixed step runs that order (`guacamole::frame`, the design doc's The frame's order).
- Fixes ship as our code in the VM or a member, never as edits to the data (Method, step 4).

Rejected: a hand-written rule engine shaped like the macros (rewriting the data); transliterating the EBOOT's interpreter (the Method); waiting for AJ (decision 2).

**11. Randomness: the original's generator, written down here.**

**21. The VM's boundary with the ECS: queries for state, messages for engine work, no copy.**
chotchki (2026-10-19), on the VM's design: Bevy's event system over "a ton of lock the world queries", then "you shouldn't even need the copy in that case, bevy provides you that consistent view and then you queue up events/commands". The script phase is one ordinary system (not an exclusive one) in `FixedUpdate`, so Bevy runs everything that doesn't touch its components beside it:
- State: a member's get and set go straight to the component through the system's queries (`Query<&mut ...>`, an O(1) `get_mut` per access). Writes land at once, because the scripts read their own writes: the hurt chain lowers health and checks it in the same tick, and a later placement in the same frame reads what an earlier one wrote. Deferred `Commands` would break that, so state never goes through them.
- Engine work: what the original only starts and the scripts poll (an animation, a sequence, a slide, an effect, a sound) goes out as a Bevy message, read after the phase; spawns and despawns go through `Commands`, as Bevy requires. Its result reaches the scripts through the components on a later tick, as the original's polling expects.
- Queries: rays, shape casts and nearest-in-a-set run synchronously through Avian's `SpatialQuery` in the same system.
- The seam: `guac-script` stays pure and calls a host trait for member get and set; guacamole implements it as a `SystemParam` over those queries, the tests over plain structs.
- Per member, its box records the visibility the oracle shows: a write later ops see at once (most state), one engine work picks up next tick (a message), or a query that sees this tick's writes or not (if a script moves an actor and raycasts in the same tick, does the original's physics see the move). A member whose visibility the traces can't settle is labelled a hypothesis, as AG.16 does.

Rejected: reading through events (every script a frame behind); a mirror of the members copied in and out around the phase (a second copy of the state for no gain, as chotchki pointed out); an exclusive `&mut World` system (it would stop the rest of the frame for no ordering the queries don't already give).
