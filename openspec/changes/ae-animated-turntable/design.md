# Design

## Context

What AE builds on, as it stands on `2.0` at 7c94178:

- **The figure list** is AA's interim catalogue: [`native_catalogue.rs`](../../../crates/guac-server/src/native_catalogue.rs) reads [`data/figures.json`](../../../data/figures.json) and `data/images/` under `DATA_ROOT` at boot before the bind, so a missing [`figures.json`](../../../data/figures.json) fails boot. Its ids are the tag form `<toy type>-<variant>` with the variant identity-masked by `VARIANT_IDENTITY_MASK = 0x0BFF` ([`crates/core/src/figure.rs`](../../../crates/core/src/figure.rs)). Two other readers lean on it: AB's intro index takes its base names as the fallback for the intros the voice-over folders don't map ([`extraction.rs`](../../../crates/guacamole/src/extraction.rs) `fallback_names`), and the placement carries its portrait path to the TV card. guacamole's database keys `figure_usage` rows and saved portal layouts by those ids ([`crates/guac-server/migrations/`](../../../crates/guac-server/migrations/)).
- **Extraction** (AB.9) runs on the first Giants pick: identity (3.3 s disk-cold, AB.14), then ffmpeg is located, THEN the intro index and the transcode. A missing ffmpeg fails the whole run today; cards keep working only because the catalogue doesn't come from the extract. IR is v1 ([`crates/guac-ir/src/lib.rs`](../../../crates/guac-ir/src/lib.rs)), a character table per toy type.
- **The TV** ([`tv.rs`](../../../crates/guacamole/src/tv.rs)) is `bevy_ui` on one `Camera2d`, rebuilding the whole screen tree on a phone-count change, with the portal's cards in two rows of four inside the 6% inset. The `guacamole` binary compiles Bevy's `ui` collection only; `hero_view` ([`examples/hero_view.rs`](../../../crates/guacamole/examples/hero_view.rs)) is the one 3D code, a dev example that decodes Eruptor off the disc in memory, GPU-skins him with Alchemy's inverse joints and draws `StandardMaterial` with his colour map alone.
- **The research** this design leans on, all outside the repo: the material model and texture roles ([`ae-materials`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-materials/FINDINGS.md)), tfbmulti2 as one function of 14 bits with its quirks ([`ae-shader`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-shader/FINDINGS.md)), the colour comparison and its refute pass ([`ae-color`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-color/FINDINGS.md), [`VERIFY.md`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-color/VERIFY.md)), the roster ([`ae-roster`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-roster/FINDINGS.md)), the toy table on a research VM ([`aj-vm`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/aj-vm/FINDINGS.md)) and the gameplay camera and light rig AC.13's savestate gave ([`ac13/savestate`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ac13/savestate/FINDINGS.md)). The research viewer that drew PARITY for the colour comparison ([`renders/README.md`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ae-color/renders/README.md)) is the existence proof for decisions 9 and 11: its WGSL tfbmulti2 into an `Rgba8Unorm` target matched the reference model on 24,144 of 24,144 channels.

Three of chotchki's calls are inputs here, not questions: variant ids take the disc's bits, Series 2 and LightCore are separate figures (both 2026-10-18, quoted in [proposal.md](proposal.md), which has the why) and two looks, PARITY and REMASTER, come from one WESL shader. What AE must do is in the specs.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- `guacamole` reads no wiki data: every figure, name, element, portrait and intro fallback name comes from the user's disc.
- One shader implementation of the character-shading spec, reviewable against the spec without Ghidra open, with a second, independent implementation (CPU, in tests) to check it against.
- PARITY checkable against the oracle at the frame level; REMASTER switchable by a key, so chotchki judges it on a real display rather than off a table.
- The Mac's renderer floor as a number before anyone builds the Enhanced look on it.

**Non-Goals:**
- Anything a level brings: level geometry and light rigs (AG), effect props, particles, `tfbwarp`/`tfbgrass` materials, levels' reflections and the shadow caster. The shader implements the shadow-receive and point-light terms the spec writes down, because they're a material's own; the turntable turns them off.
- Upgrades, upgrade art and figure progress on the card (AH, AK). Every figure draws fresh.
- Any animation but the idle loop: no clip blending, no additive layering (AC's open items on crossfades and the 262 ADDITIVE clips stay open).
- The Enhanced look (the upscale pack, modern lighting): its own line in [`openspec/backlog.md`](../../backlog.md). AE measures its budget and builds the linear-light REMASTER it starts from.
- The TV-UI move to `bevy_ui_widgets`/`bevy_input_focus` ([`tv-ui/PLAN.md`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/tv-ui/PLAN.md)). Decision 14 keeps the turntables out of the UI tree so AE doesn't need it.
- Windows. The code stays cross-platform; nothing is checked off the Mac (the Windows/HTPC bring-up, [`openspec/backlog.md`](../../backlog.md)).
- The couch. AE's by-eye checks run on the Mac's own display; the TV pass line in the backlog takes what only the TV can show.

## Decisions

**1. The roster is read statically off the disc now; AJ's VM reproduces it later.**
Four sources, all through `guac-formats` (ae-roster section 1):
- the toy table: the call sites of three macros in `character/init_setup.bld`'s `Init_Setup_Minions.ai` script, "Portal - Define Object Level Load" (77 calls, DEBUG 999 among them and dropped), "Portal - Define Legendary Alt Level Load" (15) and "Portal - Define Alt Character" (5). Each call's arguments are a literal or a handle; the reader follows the handles (`StringInfo`, `ValueInfo`, the `Minion_Names` set plus its explicit index, `global.bld` imports) and applies the sets each flag selects, the way the macro bodies do (LightCore: toys 7, 9, 20, 29, 100, 106, 108, 113; Series 2: the 24 with "has known repose");
- names: `permanent/global.bld`'s 12 `LanguagePackInfo` packs, the n-th UTF-16BE string joined to the n-th `StringInfo` of the archive's `level.bld` in address order, refused on a count mismatch;
- `global.bld` imports: export id = root-list position + 0x14, because ids are a running index and `permanent/bootstrap.bld`'s 19 roots come first (aj-vm correction 3, which also names the six ids under 0x14);
- portraits: `misc/ui_collection_champions.bld`'s 123 `tfbSpriteInfo` → `AnimatableTextureDataInfo` → `igImage2`, each a 256x256 BC3 circle.

The research VM ran the same table through the real macros and built the same 123 rows (aj-vm, Run 1), so the static reader has a second derivation to agree with already, and AJ's first disc test is that its VM reproduces this reader's output. Rejected: waiting for AJ's VM (it's M5, after AG and AH; the kids would keep the wiki list for four rungs); pulling a minimal VM forward into AE (the toy table needs only three engine members, but the VM's flow rules are AJ's to specify, and speccing them now for one script means speccing them again at AJ); a hand-written table (a third copy of data the disc already has). Reading a macro's arguments and replaying what its body does with them IS a narrow rewrite of script logic, which the Method says not to do, so this is chotchki's call (Open question 10).

**2. Figure ids carry the subtype bits Giants reads; the identity mask stays the product's.**
"Save - Get Tag Subtype Properties" reads the tag's subtype word bits 0-11: deco id in 0-7, LightCore in 9, legendary in 10, Series 2 in 11, each flag kept only when the toy is in the matching set (ae-roster section 1; aj-vm confirmed the membership code). A Guacamole id's variant is that word as Giants keeps it: 0x0400 | deco for an alt deco, 0x0800 Series 2, 0x0200 LightCore, 0 for a base; Legendary Chill is 0x0603 (its portrait is the Legendary-LightCore one). The ids stay `<toy type>-<variant>` strings, so the phone, the routes and the placement seam don't change shape; what changes is that the Native side never runs a variant through `0x0BFF` (the type that carries it on the Guacamole side says so in its name, AE.4). The product keeps its mask, its pack index and its ids.

12 AA-era ids change (ae-roster, Every difference from AA): 0x0802 → 0x0402 for Polar Whirlwind and Royal Double Trouble, 0x0803 → 0x0403 for Legendary Ignitor, Slam Bam and Stealth Elf, 0x0202 → 0x0402 for Granite Crusher, Scarlet Ninjini and Gnarly Tree Rex, 0x0002 → 0x0402 for Molten Hot Dog and Punch Pop Fizz, 0x0203 → 0x0603 for Legendary Chill and 0x0203 → 0x0403 for Legendary Bouncer, each on its own toy type. A new migration in guacamole's set rewrites them in `figure_usage` and in `sessions.last_portal_layout_json` (the layout is a JSON blob, rewritten by exact id string; every changed id is unique by toy type). A new file, never an edit to an applied one: AA.11 found sqlx refusing a database whose applied migration's checksum changed.

**3. Names, elements, portraits and the language.**
- Names: English (language id 6) is what the phone and TV show. The 49 character names and 15 variant words are the same in all 12 packs; items, locations and elements are translated (ae-roster section 2). The IR keeps all 12, so a language setting later is a settings row, not a re-extraction (Open question 11).
- A variant's name is the disc's variant word, a space, then the character's name: "Polar Whirlwind", "Series 2 Whirlwind", "LightCore Hex", "Golden Dragonfire Cannon". The disc has no combined string; the game builds a subtitle instead ("Get Subtype Strings for Toy"), and the separate-figure call means the name has to carry it. A separate-toy variant (Dark Spyro, the four Spyro's Adventure legendaries) is a base on its own toy type with the name the disc gives it. Variant group = the character's name, variant tag = the word, as the phone's collection already groups them.
- Element: the disc's literal or `ElementIndex_*` value (0 Magic, 1 Water, 2 Air, 3 Undead, 4 Tech, 5 Fire, 6 Earth, 7 Life). Magic items and adventure-pack pieces carry 0 (the macro's default) and show no element; Volcanic Vault's 5 shows Fire. That 0 means "none" on those is the research's hypothesis, kept because wrong is worse than missing (AA's rule) and the collection screen in the oracle can settle it any time.
- Category from the sets: `giant loads` → Giant, `Item_Loads` → Item, `Location_Loads` plus Volcanic Vault → AdventurePack, `pet level loads` → Sidekick, the rest Figure.
- Portraits: decoded once at extraction to PNG (the phone can't show BC3; the TV loads the same file), under the extract dir like every derived image.

**4. The catalogue lives in the extract and is published when it's known.**
- The server's figure list becomes shared and replaceable after boot, the way AA.15 made the game list (`GameList`). Boot builds no catalogue; `Catalogue::Native` loses its [`figures.json`](../../../data/figures.json) read, `DATA_ROOT` stops being a key guacamole reads, and a release run no longer needs `SKYLANDER_APP_DIR`. The list and its id index live in the state both binaries share, so every reader moves onto the shared list: the routes, the driver worker's index copy, Kaos's swap and the product's `sky_edit` (two lookups in [`sky_edit.rs`](../../../crates/server/src/sky_edit.rs)). The product fills it once at boot from the pack as it does now and never replaces it.
- The game picker's box art is wiki data too: `/api/games/<serial>/image` serves [`data/games/`](../../../data/games/)`<serial>.png` from `DATA_ROOT`, and the Removals line takes those files at G1. guacamole answers that route from the found dump's own `PS3_GAME/ICON0.PNG` instead (the disc's icon, beside the `PARAM.SFO` the dump scan already reads), read in place and never copied; a dump without one gets a 404 and the card's gradient, as a miss does today. The product's route doesn't change.
- Where the list comes from: at start, when setup has a Giants dump and a CURRENT extraction exists for it, its roster loads on a background thread (no phone can have picked a game yet); otherwise it lands with the first stage of the extraction the Giants pick starts (decision 5). Until then `/api/figures` answers an empty list, and `/api/status` (which the phone already reads for `app`, AA.18) carries a roster state, `reading`, `unrecognized`, `unreadable` or `ready`: the phone's collection shows the first three as a note and polls until `ready` (spec: within 2 s of the roster landing). The product's status never carries one.
- "Resume last setup?" places by id, so it waits for the roster: an unlock before it lands holds the resume until it does, and a saved id the roster doesn't know is skipped and logged rather than failing the resume.
- [`native_catalogue.rs`](../../../crates/guac-server/src/native_catalogue.rs), its row rules and its hand-written supplement are deleted; AA.14's four rows are on the disc with portraits (ae-roster confirmed them). The intro index's name fallback reads the roster's English base names. After this nothing in `guacamole` opens `data/`; the files stay for the product until G1.

**5. Extraction order: identity, roster, models, then ffmpeg and the intros.**
The roster and portraits come first (three archives), then each character model and its textures, then ffmpeg is located and the intros are indexed and transcoded. The roster is published to the server the moment its stage completes, so a phone can place figures while the rest runs, and a missing ffmpeg leaves the roster and the models in place with only the intros unavailable (AB's ffmpeg requirement keeps holding). Each stage writes through AB's per-file resume, and the manifest's completion mark still means the whole extraction. The TV's progress band names the stage. Time and size per stage are measured on the Mac (AE.3, AE.6).

**6. IR v2 and the extract layout; no glTF.**
IR goes to v2, which re-extracts every existing extract once (AB's manifest rule):
- `roster`: one record per figure: toy type, kind (base, alt, Series 2, LightCore), deco id, the subtype word, name key with its 12 texts, variant-word key and text, element, category, load file, portrait file.
- `models/<load file>`: the hero model of that character archive (the `tfbPhysicsModel` hero walk `hero_view` already does): segments with positions, normals, tangents with handedness, up to three UV sets, vertex colours and four (bone, weight) skin influences; the skeleton (names, parents, Alchemy inverse joints, the bind AC.17 settled); the segments a fresh figure draws (decision 7); per segment its material (decision 9's masks, textures by role, parameters, render states, UV tracks); the idle clip as Havok spline data (decision 8).
- `textures/<content hash>.ktx2`: AC.6's writer, original BC blocks, deduplicated by content (character bundles repeat each other's textures).
- `portraits/<figure id>.png`.
- `rig`: the 3D-UI light rig, `global.bld`'s three `_spriteLight` `DirectionalLightInfo`s (decision 14, Open question 4).

`LAYOUT_VERSION` goes to 2 with it, since the layout gains those entries (either bump alone would stale a v1 extract; both keep the manifest honest). RON in dev for diffing, postcard in release, as v1. The design doc's extractor section says meshes, skeletons and animations go to glTF; AE doesn't, because tfbmulti2's material has no glTF form (it would ride in `extras` and Bevy's loader would build a `StandardMaterial` we throw away) and a glTF clip means resampling Havok's splines into keys. Bevy builds a `Mesh` from IR directly (`hero_view` already builds one from the disc's decode). A glTF export for inspecting models in Blender can come back as a dev tool. Chotchki's call since it reverses a written line (Open question 9).

The game-magic guard ([`crates/guac-extract/src/magic.rs`](../../../crates/guac-extract/src/magic.rs)) learns KTX2's identifier: the extract is full of disc textures in that container, and the repo holds no KTX2 (test fixtures are built in code).

**7. What a fresh figure draws, and which clip is the idle.**
A fresh figure draws only the segments the game draws with no upgrades bought. Eruptor's archive also holds his upgrade spikes (`EruptorMatte`), which AC.13's frames of his no-upgrade figure leave out: they drew segments 122, 123 and 135 of his model. The rule that picks a fresh figure's segments comes from a census over every hero model (the scene graph's switch and blend-select structure, the material and segment names), is checked on Eruptor against AC.13's draws and on more characters against oracle screenshots as AF.5 makes them cheap, and is written into the turntable spec's terms (a model shows what the game draws with no upgrades). Same for the idle: the clip each character's animation set names as its idle, from a census; `Eruptor_Idle` is AC.17's validated case. A character the rule can't settle draws its full hero model and is listed in AE.5's box, never guessed silently.

**8. Clips are evaluated, not resampled.**
The IR stores each idle as the spline data AC.8 decodes (knots, control points, per-track quantization), and one evaluator in `guac-ir` samples it at any time, shared by the extractor's tests and the runtime. guacamole evaluates the pose on the CPU each frame, writes bone transforms and lets Bevy's GPU skinning apply Alchemy's inverse joints (u8 weights / 255, four per vertex, the same blend the SPU does). That's exact at any frame rate and compact, on the decode AC.17 already proved within 3.4e-7 of the diagonal. Rejected: resampling to per-frame keys for `AnimationClip` (lossy between keys and bigger, where the original evaluates the spline at arbitrary times).

**9. One WESL shader, a shader def per feature bit.**
The material key is (pixel mask, vertex mask, look, render states). `specialize` turns each set bit into a shader def that the WESL source tests with `@if` (Bevy 0.20 passes a `.wesl` asset's shader defs to its `@if` conditions; `bevy_pbr`'s own `lightmap.wesl` does the same). The mask is ae-materials' selector: the material's base bits plus the scene's runtime bits outside its ignore mask (shadows bit 8, fog bit 10, point lights 11-13 thermometer-coded). The 11 bits characters use are implemented: layers 2, 3 and 4, normal map, spec map, directional lighting, receives shadows, fog, three point-light counts, plus the vertex side's vertex colour. Bits 6, 7 and 9 (rim by position, gloss map, reflections) are never set on a character and vertex bit 11 (skinning) is never compiled (ae-shader section 4), so the extractor refuses a hero material carrying one with a named error (the census in AE.5 confirms none does), and AG adds them when a level needs them.
- Lighting stays in view space: the saturated eye vector and the sphere-map lookup are view-space by construction (ae-shader, Vertex side), and lighting a fixed rig in view space is what a turntable wants anyway (decision 14).
- The vertex stage writes the interpolants the spec needs and nothing else: UV sets through their 2x3 transforms, the eye-space normal, tangent and bitangent (cross(N, T) × handedness), eye position and fog factor, the eye vector under the exact condition the spec states (spec map AND (fog OR point light OR rim by position)), vertex colour or half grey.
- f32 throughout. ae-shader measured an fp32 evaluation against RSX-like fp16 on Eruptor's 0x41C: 99.9% of channels within 1/255, the worst 2.5/255.

**10. A second implementation in Rust, from the spec.**
A CPU reference model of the character-shading spec, written from the spec text and not from the shader (as ae-color's refute pass rebuilt the shader from the findings' prose and matched to 9.8e-7). Unit tests pin each quirk the spec names (no eye vector without fog, the per-component eye clamp, `abs` in the normal's Z, x1.0079 for neutral tints, the 1e-5 floor, an exponent under 1, the alpha ramp at -0.001, the rim's opposite split, point lights unscaled by albedo). The GPU lane renders synthetic quads through every mask the roster uses and requires the 8-bit output to equal the model's `round(clamp(x) · 255)` on every channel, the research viewer's check, run on the Mac as an `#[ignore]`d test (no CI lane has a GPU).

**11. PARITY renders into an `Rgba8Unorm` image and is shown without a second encode.**
Each PARITY camera renders into its own `Rgba8Unorm` image: LDR, `Tonemapping::None`, `DebandDither::Disabled`, `Msaa::Off`. Bevy takes a render target's format as its main texture format, so the material's output lands as stored, each channel clamped on its own, and blending happens on stored values as the PS3's does (881 character materials blend, 150 additively). The card shows the image through a small UI material that decodes the sample from sRGB, so the window's sRGB encode gives back the same bytes; the GPU lane checks the window's bytes against the image's at 1:1.

Rejected: decoding in the material and writing into Bevy's usual `Rgba8UnormSrgb` main texture (the bytes survive, but every blend then happens in linear light); `CompositingSpace::Srgb` (Bevy 0.20 honors it on `Camera2d` views only and resolves a 3D camera's request to linear, per `bevy_render` 0.20's compositing-space resolver). PARITY means the arithmetic, not the PS3's 1280x720 or its aliasing: cards render at their size on the TV, and the oracle comparison renders at 1280x720 like the game.

**12. REMASTER: the same shader under `@if(REMASTER)`, in linear light.**
- Every colour the disc stores (the lights' ambient, diffuse and specular colours, fog and replace colours) is held once as a `bevy_color::Srgba` built with `Srgba::rgb_u8`, and the material's uniforms take `LinearRgba::from` it in REMASTER and its raw components in PARITY.
- Tints and vertex colours are NOT colours to decode: they're multipliers centred on 128 (×4·tint·material tint, ×2·vertex colour, so neutral is ×1.0079 and ×1). Decoding a neutral tint gives 0.216, and 4 · 0.216² scales every part by 0.186 instead of 1.0079, about 5.4× darker. ae-color's linear knob, the measured options (c)-(e) below, kept both as plain factors, so REMASTER does too. A non-neutral tint (Storm Giant's yellow bolt) in linear light wasn't measured; AE.12 renders one tinted material in both looks and records it.
- Colour, spec and rim textures sample through `*UnormSrgb` views in REMASTER, so the hardware decodes before filtering; normal maps stay `Unorm` in both looks.
- The camera is HDR (`Hdr`, an `Rgba16Float` main texture) with `Msaa::Sample4` and renders into an `Rgba8UnormSrgb` image the card shows directly. The tonemapper and any fix on top are Open question 1; until chotchki picks, the default is the recommendation and a dev key cycles the measured options on the TV so he can pick by eye.
- The game's glow chain (decision 13) runs in REMASTER too, on linear values; AE.11 builds it in PARITY and AE.12 carries it over. Bevy's own `Bloom` stays out of the looks; the floor bench is the only place AE turns it on.

**13. The game's glow, read from the captures AC.13 already took.**
An RSX capture keeps the whole command stream and register state of its frame even though its render targets come back zero (AC.12), so AC.13's three captures of Eruptor already hold the bloom chain: `TFBMulti2_for_bloom` (writing the glow mask; ae-shader disassembled it), `tfb_bloom_extract`, the blur passes and `tfb_bloom_combine`, with their target sizes, write masks, blend states and constants. AE.11 reads them with `rrc-dump`, writes the chain into the spec's Glow requirement in our words and implements it in both looks. The same reading identifies the two extra passes on EBOOT fragment programs that look like his yellow glow (AC.13's hypothesis). VERIFY.md's unexplained gap on his rock plates (oracle L 0.68 against PARITY's 0.47) is the expected sign of that chain, so its comparison numbers before and after are recorded.

**14. A turntable per occupied slot, outside the UI tree.**
- Each occupied slot with a model gets a scene of its own: the model's entities on their own `RenderLayers`, a camera that sees only that layer and renders into the slot's image (decision 11 or 12 by look). A system reconciles these scenes from `PortalCards` by (slot, placement), so a UI rebuild never respawns a model; the card's image node is rebuilt pointing at the slot's persistent image. That keeps AE independent of the TV-UI move (whose TV.3, persistent cells, would make the image node persist too).
- The UI's `Camera2d` gets `IsDefaultUiCamera` (the TV-UI plan's own note for when a 3D camera arrives); the turntable cameras target images, never the window.
- Framing: the camera fits the bounds of the idle loop (sampled over the clip, so a swinging arm doesn't leave the card) at a fixed downward pitch, giants and small characters alike. The model turns about its vertical axis at a steady rate; the rig is fixed to the camera, like studio lights around a real turntable.
- Lights and scene bits: the rig is Open question 4 (recommended: the 3D-UI sprite rig, from the IR's `rig`). Fog is ON with its distances beyond the camera: the fog factor is 0, but the eye vector exists, so a spec-mapped part shows the sheen it shows in the level; shadows and point lights are off.
- The portrait shows until the model is ready and stays when it can't be (spec); a model that fails to load is logged by figure and reason and never touches another slot.

**15. Every pipeline the roster needs is compiled before the portal shows.**
macOS compiles a render pipeline inside the frame that first uses it (design doc, Renderer), so at start, and again when an extraction lands, a hidden offscreen pass draws one triangle per material key the roster's models use, in both looks, plus the present materials, before the Portal screen first shows. AE.14 measures the worst TV frame over the first placement after a start and models appearing, and AE.15 a look switch once its key exists, each against the spec's 50 ms.

**16. The look is a setting.**
A `look` row in guacamole's `settings` table (`parity` or `remaster`), `guacamole --look <parity|remaster>` storing it and starting (the way `--setup <folder>` stores), `GUAC_LOOK` as the dev override (like `GUAC_DUMP_DIRS`) and the `L` key on the PC toggling and storing it while the portal shows (the PC's keyboard is the grown-up's; the kids hold pads and phones). A first start draws REMASTER (Open question 2). The phone gets no switch in AE.

**17. The oracle comparison.**
`hero_view` moves onto the new material (`--look`) and gains AC.18's camera options (the capture's view-projection, the output size), so it can render Eruptor at an oracle frame's camera, idle time, light rig and fog, all read at run time from the capture dir and never committed. The reference frame is a pair AF.5 makes unattended: a `SAVESTATE` (camera, rig, fog and the pose solve, as AC.13's gave) and a `SCREENSHOT` at the same flip. The metric is FLIP through AF.10's implementation. A threshold set from the first comparison can't fail that comparison, so it's a regression bound, not a parity verdict: it's recorded with the residuals the comparison names (the shadowed area, any glow not yet drawn) and later changes can't drift past it. Whether that first number is close enough is read off the residual breakdown, ae-color's Oklch hero statistics beside it (chroma, lightness, the R+G clip share, the lava hue; rock and lava regions apart) and AF.12's frame floor at that checkpoint if it has landed, and it's chotchki's at AE.18. ae-color's g5 screenshot doesn't serve: its pose isn't the solved one and it registers at r = 0.80. The frame AC.13 drew used variant 0x51C (shadows on), and the game's shadow map isn't in a capture (render targets are zero), so the comparison draws 0x41C and reports the shadowed area as a known residual.

**18. The renderer floor, as a bench.**
`examples/turntable_bench`: Eruptor alone and a full portal of eight characters, on a REMASTER HDR camera with cascaded shadow maps on one directional light, `ScreenSpaceAmbientOcclusion`, `TemporalAntiAliasing` (MSAA off, as TAA requires) and `Bloom`, at 3840x2160 offscreen and at the Mac display's own resolution, every pipeline pre-warmed. `RenderDiagnosticsPlugin` gives per-pass GPU time over a few hundred frames after warm-up, recorded with the load average. CSM and SSAO feed Bevy's PBR lighting, which our material doesn't read yet, so the bench draws the decoded models with `StandardMaterial`: it measures the stack's cost on this geometry at this resolution, not a look. The TV's own cost (eight turntables in each look) is measured beside it.

**19. No disc, no GPU: what runs in `cargo test`.**
Parsers, the roster reader, the IR, the evaluator, the reference model, the material key and the reconcile system are unit-tested on synthetic data built in code (`guac-formats`' IGZ builder, IR constructed directly). Anything that reads the dump is `#[ignore]`d and takes `GUAC_GIANTS_DUMP`; anything that needs a GPU is an `#[ignore]`d lane run on the Mac. The e2e suite under `E2E_APP=guacamole` stops getting figures from the wiki data, so under `test-hooks` the harness hands guacamole a synthetic extract built in code: a roster of a handful of named figures and one synthetic model (a few triangles, one bone, a two-key clip, a flat-colour texture). Names are facts rather than game bytes, and nothing of the disc's goes into a fixture.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [The fresh-figure rule is wrong for some characters, so a card shows upgrade parts a fresh figure doesn't have] → decision 7's census plus oracle screenshots of more than Eruptor; any character the rule can't settle is listed, not guessed.
- [The glow chain or the extra glow passes don't come out of the captures readably] → PARITY lands with the main pass and whatever of the chain is read, and the comparison records the residual (VERIFY.md's rock-plate gap is the expected size). AF.5's `RSXCAP` gives fresh captures if AC.13's aren't enough.
- [REMASTER's look changes after chotchki sees it on the TV] → it's a key and a setting, the measured options stay switchable in dev builds and the default is one constant.
- [Linear light changes pixels that never clipped (VERIFY.md: rock C 0.107 → 0.081)] → that's Open question 1's trade-off, measured, stated and his call.
- [The roster is empty on any dump guacamole doesn't recognize, the HTPC's included if it hashes differently] → by design (Open question 5); the bring-up's line already plans a second known-dump entry for that case.
- [The static toy-table reader drifts from what the scripts do] → the research VM already agrees on 123 of 123; AJ's VM must reproduce the reader's output before it replaces it.
- [The id migration misses a place an id is stored] → AE.4's test seeds every table and the layout blob with the 12 old ids and requires the new ones after; the resume skips (and logs) any id the roster doesn't know.
- [IR v2's first extraction takes longer, and the extract grows] → measured per stage and recorded (AE.3, AE.6); resumable per file, and the roster lands first.
- [The 3D Bevy features slow `.githooks/pre-push` and grow the binary] → measured cold and warm on the Mac, as AA.10 did for `ui` (AE.8); the remedy, if it's painful, is its own change.
- [macOS pipeline compiles hitch the TV] → decision 15's pre-warm and AE.14's measurement against 50 ms.
- [A turntable material binds an L8 texture, whose untile AC.21 hasn't settled] → retired by AC.21 (2026-10-18): the disc's only L8 images are the depth-of-field pass's two focus mappers, which no material binds; AE.5's census still confirms it per figure.
- [PARITY reproduces RPCS3, not a real PS3] → by construction: COL1's saturation is RPCS3's hardware-test reading (ae-shader open item 4), and the oracle IS RPCS3. The design doc says parity means "as seen through the oracle".
- [The Mac's display isn't the TV] → sRGB on the Mac's panel is measured (VERIFY.md: the display path adds nothing there); a TV in its own picture mode is the TV pass's.
- [A test fixture smuggles disc bytes into the repo] → fixtures are built in code from numbers we chose; the guard checks every staged blob, KTX2 included (decision 6).
- [A decoded portrait gets committed] → portraits are PNG, which the guard can't flag (the repo has PNGs of its own); the extract root's refusal of any git work tree keeps them out of a checkout, and AE.0 checks `git ls-files` against the extract dir before the last commit.

## Migration Plan

- Land order: the roster first (AE.1-AE.4), which retires the wiki reading on its own and is kid-visible (123 figures, every one with its portrait); then the models, the shader, the looks and the turntable.
- Data: IR v2 makes every extract stale, and the next Giants pick re-extracts (a cache, by design). guacamole's database takes one new migration for the 12 ids.
- Archive order: AE's deltas MODIFY requirements that live in AA's and AB's unarchived deltas, so `openspec archive` refuses AE until AA and AB have archived (validate passes now, with an INFO saying so). AE stands on both anyway.
- Rollback: a revert, plus deleting the extract dir. The rewritten ids stay in guacamole's database, and an older guacamole's resume drops the ones its catalogue doesn't know (a dev database only: nothing has shipped).
