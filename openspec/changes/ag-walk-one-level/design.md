## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A kid's character walking chapter 1's opening on the Mac with an 8BitDo pad, moving the way the original moves by every check the oracle can make.
- Eruptor walking on the TV as early as the conditions allow (AG.21), before the envelope work and before chotchki's answer on the script stand-ins.
- Every behaviour AG writes from observation of a script is a spec requirement AJ's VM must pass before it deletes the stand-in, all of them in one list.
- The oracle as a per-frame recorder, so the movement and camera specs and the model replay stand on traces of the original, not on the decompile.

**Non-Goals:**
- Anything scripted past the stand-ins: enemies, NPCs, critters, breakables, Titan feats, tutorial prompts, the intro cinematic (`213_Intro_IGC`) and the HUD (AH, AJ, AM).
- Rigid-body physics. Nothing in the tutorial's live world is dynamic.
- Co-op (AL). One hero; a second character waits.
- What the collision materials mean (ag-motion's open question 4). AG keeps each triangle's material in the IR and tallies which ones the opening area has (AG.7), so AJ's surface handlers have what they need.
- Falling out of the level: no death volume is in reach and every edge with nothing below stops the hero (decision 15), so AJ's death scripts take it.
- Music, ambience and footsteps (decision 16).
- Any level but 213; the hub and the PvP arenas (AI's).
- The Home menu (design doc Open question 1) and AP.7's directional moves, still the backlog's.
- Windows; the couch (the TV pass in [`openspec/backlog.md`](../../backlog.md)).
- Leans, random idles and footstep tags (AJ's locomotion scripts).

## Decisions

Rejected: waiting for AJ (M2 would be one hero for three rungs for no gain in correctness). Open question 1.

- The dependency: avian3d 0.8 from crates.io; until it publishes, Avian's `main` at `1070759b2145` ("Update to Bevy 0.20 (#1084)") as a git dependency pinned by rev, never #1070's `34c9b008d2f4`. Read on 2026-10-18: #1070 closed unmerged, the maintainer writing "Done in #1084, which is based on this but fixes CI and bumps the dependencies to released versions. I merged it to main already, and will try to get the actual Avian 0.8 release done tomorrow"; `main` builds avian3d `0.8.0-dev` on Bevy 0.20, parry3d 0.31 and `bevy_transform_interpolation` 0.6; crates.io's newest is 0.7.0, on Bevy 0.19. chotchki's pick stands; the branch the developer recommends moved.
- What's used, all on `main` at that rev: `SpatialQuery::{cast_shape, cast_ray, shape_intersections, project_point}`, `contact_manifolds`, `Collider::trimesh_with_config` with `TrimeshFlags::FIX_INTERNAL_EDGES` (which replaces the original's welding words), static colliders for the level and kinematic or sensor colliders for placements. No `RigidBody::Dynamic` anywhere: the live tutorial's `hkpWorld` holds one static body, 57 shape phantoms and 9 AABB phantoms, and no dynamic body (ag-motion verdict 3).
- The seam lives in a new pure crate, `crates/guac-motion` (no Bevy): a shape cast returning the time of impact and the contact planes there, contact manifolds within a distance at a pose, a ray cast returning the triangle it hit (so its material), overlaps. Two implementations: Avian's in `guacamole` (an adapter over `SpatialQuery` inside the movement system) and parry3d's in `guac-motion` over static trimeshes and convex primitives, which runs every controller test without Bevy.
- Both are tested, and both are replayed. AG.4 runs every seam case through Avian's side in a headless app (Avian needs no renderer), AG.16 runs the controller's open-ground and wall cases there too, and AG.18 replays the oracle recordings through both, recording their difference: the oracle checks the Parry twin directly and the Avian side the TV runs through it. A `guacamole` built on the Parry twin alone, if Avian's schedule or API bites, would depart from chotchki's pick, so it waits for his yes.
- Rejected: Avian's solver and Rapier (no dynamics to solve); a MOPP reader (Parry builds its own tree from the explicit triangles, ag-levels).
- Cost: Avian and Parry join the `guacamole` binary's build and so `.githooks/pre-push`; AG.4 measures it cold and warm, as AA.10 did for Bevy.

**4. The controller: written from the spec, every mechanism observed first.**
ag-motion section 1 read the original's movement out of the code: the velocity model (facing toward the stick, turning scaled by the stick's strength squared and the angle left, top speed shrinking while turning, the heading gate of 135° moving, 15° from rest and 45° without input, deceleration always and acceleration on top, a soft speed cap, gravity to terminal); the proxy loop (at most 10 casts a step, a manifold that drops near-duplicate planes, a frictionless plane solve, no upward gain); support by a downward probe at 45°; the snap to 0.01 m above ground within 0.375 m; tilt toward the ground at up to 120°/s; the downhill hug rays (from 2 m up, 4 m long) and the ground finder. That reading is a map of what to observe, not a spec: the Method makes the decompiler "a reading aid for a mechanism the oracle already showed, never a source of code". So AG.8-AG.10 observe each mechanism through O5 before AG.16 builds it, and each lands as a hero-movement requirement with its number: the gates (from-rest walks at 0°, 14° and 16° off his facing, reversals, a stick let go mid-turn), the turn law (full and part stick), the ramp (from rest at two strengths), the snap (the deepest drop he stays grounded on), the tilt (its rate across a slope change), steps (the highest he gets up), walls (no height gained, the distance kept, the slide speed), slopes, falls. What the oracle can't separate (the plane dedupe, the hug rays against the snap, the ground finder) AG.16 labels a hypothesis with the reading behind it, and AG.18's replay is where a wrong one shows. Shapes come from `CollisionInfo`: full extents in feet times the scale, 0.1 m smaller all round with a 0.05 m convex radius, the pivot at the bottom centre, checked against the three shapes ag-motion verified live (a push block, Norticus, a sheep), against AG.8's live read of Eruptor's sphere (ag-motion's state had no hero) and against the gate door's box, which unlike the three isn't symmetric in x and z.

Why the original's shape over a built-in: what decides the oracle's branch outcomes (which planes count as one, how the solve slides, that nothing gains height) is known and small, and the model replay (decision 7) only compares like with like if the loop is the original's. Why the traces and not the reading, three times over:
- The acceleration. AF.9's first 30 flips from rest covered 2.97-2.99 m; the model as read (acceleration minus deceleration, 80 ft/s² net) predicts 2.74-2.82 m given AF.11's spread of first frames, and acceleration alone 3.00-3.05 m ([`accel.txt`](/Users/chotchki/workspace/guacamole-research/ag-propose/accel.txt)). Neither fits; the integration order (position before or after the velocity update, about 6.8 mm a frame apart while he speeds up) is AG.1's and AG.8's to settle, with an O5 from-rest recording's own steps, since AF.9 never recorded its first frame's.
- The 15° gate sits exactly on the start. The camera looks across the ground at 15° and Eruptor faces 0°, so the stick held forward points 15.00° off his facing, the from-rest gate's edge; which side of "more than 15°" it falls on decides his first frame. AG.8 settles it from frames 1-3, the requirement says whether 15° itself counts, and AG.16 tests 15° ± 0.01°.
- The thresholds disagree. `checkSupport` reads 45° and the velocity model's leave-the-ground test normal.y < 0.69 (46.4°), so ground in that band is the oracle's to settle; AG.16 tests "doesn't hold" at 50° and adds a band case only after AG.9 measures one. And the idle height: the ground under the spawn is 43.3777, flat to 0.11°, and AF.9's idle pivot 43.3827, 5.0 mm up, which the 0.01 m snap as read doesn't give; AG.18 settles him from the waypoint and either lands within 1e-4 m or AG.8's recordings explain the gap.

As measured (AG.8, 2026-10-19, 59 O5b recordings from AF's fresh state on the level ground the spawn sits in, every game frame): the read model isn't what walks Eruptor. His `ActorParameters` hold 10,000 ft/s, no acceleration or deceleration, 10,000°/s and motion type 2 at rest and through every walk, changing only for the frames an action button plays (AG.1 read the swap at the South press as walking), so his walk runs on numbers held elsewhere, `Speed_Slow`'s live 21.5 ft/s among them. Each game frame, with the stick's strength S from the per-axis dead zone: the walk takes the stick's direction against the view at once, whatever his facing or speed; the speed rises by 160 ft/s² × S × the step up to S × 21.5 ft/s (a speed over a weaker push's top holds), and with the stick let go falls by 80 ft/s² × the step along the last direction to 0; the facing turns toward the walk the short way by 1440°/s × S × the step and freezes when let go. No 15°, 45° or 135° gate, no stop dead, no turn slowing the walk, no S² or angle factor on the turn. The rise and the turn integrate the step of the frame two before, the fall the frame before, the position its own (AG.1's order): one update at a time the law holds to 1.3e-4 m/s, 4e-3° on the facing and 4.1e-6 m on the position over 14,733 updates, and 167 pushes from rest rebuilt from their own steps cover the recorded distance to 2.2e-5 m and end within 5.3e-5 m of where the recording does. So the brief's acceleration gap closes as acceleration alone, scaled by the strength, and the reading decision 7's replay bound cites is settled; AG.16 builds this law and AG.18 feeds it each frame's step with those lags. The proxy's contact loop, the ground and slopes stay AG.9's: on the mound ahead of the spawn a part-stick walk runs faster than its level top.

As measured (AG.9, 2026-10-19, 38 O5b recordings with the hero's contacts on the watch list: his proxy's manifold, its support check and his ground): walls, slopes, the snap and the lean rest on hero-movement's Walls, Slopes, Steps and drops and He leans with the ground. On slopes to 17.3° his speed is the walk's along the ground. He rides 5 mm above level ground and 10 mm above the corridor's slopes, up and down, once the snap has set him there (5 mm after a landing), is held 10 mm above ground that falls away, and is set back from as high as 189 mm in a frame. He leans at 120°/s. Crossing onto steeper downhill ground he can lose support for a frame and land 0.88-1.36 m/s faster, which the soft cap holds: the mound's half-stick 4.41-4.49 m/s. And the walk doesn't always take the stick's way at once: 10 mm up, it steers by 160 ft/s² × S (AF.11's gate split, AG.38). Hypotheses for AG.16, the oracle not separating them in the opening area: the 10-cast cap and the plane dedupe (the manifold grew past two entries only in corners, where it moved and the watch lost it: 8 replays), the 45° and 46.4° thresholds (nothing between 26° and 73° in reach), the snap's 0.375 m reach past 189 mm, the hug rays (nothing in reach separates them from the snap: on the corridor's falls of 3.4°-17.3° he rides the snap's 10 mm, on 0.15°-1° he floats), any step height (no riser between 4 cm and 2.36 m), and what switches the walk between turning at once and steering and what sets a landing's gain (AG.38).

Rejected: Avian's `MoveAndSlide` with its per-hit callback (iterations, depenetration, a 0.01 skin and up to 20 similar planes; the callback can express no upward gain, but its solve and plane merging aren't the original's, so a branch could go the other way for reasons nobody can name; worth a measured comparison only if cheap); tnua or ahoy (someone else's feel).

**5. Time: a fixed 1/60 s step, game time defined and what "the original tick" means.**
The original integrates each frame's step: its measured frame time (`_frameDelta`, raw, which AF.11 saw spread up to 0.14 s) clamped to between half and three times the nominal 1/60 s, so [1/120, 1/20] s, times the game's time scale and the actor's (ag-motion section 1). Game time is the sum of those steps; every key, AG.1's check, AG.18's replay and AG.31's metrics use it, never flips and never the raw `_frameDelta`. At full speed a step is 1/60 s and its long frames are hitches, not design: AF.9's steady 30-flip legs covered 3.40-3.42 m, which is 29 flips at 1/60 s plus one 0.036 s flip after each `PAUSE_AT` at 6.553 m/s (3.403 m). guacamole steps the hero and the camera in `FixedUpdate` at 60 Hz with dt = 1/60 s, transforms interpolated for display (`bevy_transform_interpolation`, which comes with Avian). The design doc's "the logic stays at the original tick" then means the nominal 1/60 s; AG.33 writes that down as a definition, not a new departure. The AF.7 scripts every recording runs are keyed on flips, so the same leg covers different game time in different replays; each recording maps its script to game time from its own steps, guacamole's runs at 1/60 s, and AG.31's metrics are the time-normalised ones. **6. O5: a per-frame recorder that doesn't pause.**
AF.11 showed that observing moves the run: a pause every 60 flips added about 19 MT draws each and moved time-integrated state (the camera's resting offset after the walk, 3.05-3.23 m behind in x without the pauses against 3.33-3.55 m with them). O5 is P9 in AF decision 2's form: game-agnostic C++, Giants' addresses in our Rust. `WATCH <frames> <address> <length> ...` arms a ring; once per flip, at the flip's first pad read (O4 already keys the flip there under the pad mutex, and AF.7 counts several reads a flip), the emulator copies the named ranges into it on the guest thread doing the read. `WATCH_READ` returns the ring at a pause and `WATCH_CLEAR` drops it; ranges times frames are bounded. Whether a sample at flip N's read holds the pose of the update just before it or the one before that is AG.1's to find, by checking position steps against velocity times the clamped step under both. Whether O5 itself perturbs the run is checked with AF.11's own discriminators, which tell set A from set B where end positions can't (set A's end spread is bimodal and 1.48 m wide): MT draws since F0 by rank-sum, the camera's resting offset, the slide outcome's stop point. Rejected: `PAUSE_AT` checkpoints (they move the run); a guest dump per frame (far too slow, and PINE reads race a running game).

As built (AG.1, then AG.35): O5 is [`0005-O5-*.patch`](../../../rpcs3-patches/oracle/0005-O5-oracle-per-frame-recorder-over-the-P1-socket-GUAC.patch). `WATCH` takes up to 64 ranges (16 KiB a record, 16 MiB a ring) and a pad port; the first thing `pad_get_data` does, under `pad::g_pad_mutex`, is copy them at every read of that port (AG.1's took each flip's first read on any port), a record being the flip, the host's clock, a mask of the ranges that weren't readable, which of the port's reads in that flip it was and the bytes; `WATCH_READ` hands back the summary or 32 KiB of records a reply, at a pause only; a new session drops the watch.
